# Review 06 — The annotation programme: protocol, QA, inter-rater measurement, label efficiency

Reviewer 6 of 10. Slice: `vault/Research/Annotation and label efficiency/` + `vault/Research/Class schema/Handover/Annotation protocol evidence.md`, audited against `src/segqueue/`, `src/segtrain/metrics.py`, `server/girder_segqueue/`, `slicer/`.

---

## 1. Verdict in five lines

1. **Yes, it is a category error, and it is the most important thing in this slice.** Sim & Wright's Table 8 sizes a *categorical* kappa study; the overlap set SegQueue actually runs emits per-class **Dice** (`scoring.py` → `metrics.agreement`). There is no kappa anywhere in the codebase. §2.5's "uncomfortable" 126/503 conclusion is answering a question the platform does not ask, while the question it does ask has a different and cheaper answer that can be computed today from ImageCAS-X's published SDs.
2. **Intersection auto-accept must be deleted.** For equal-volume masks the intersection's volume *is* the Dice coefficient: it erodes 7.2% of the merged tree and 25.9%/29.1% of OM1/L-PLA by construction, at boundaries and distal tips. It also inverts its own source — FIVES used intersection as the *input* to senior review of every image, never to skip review.
3. **§1.5's mitigation is aimed in the wrong direction and the shipped tool enforces the bias.** If the ImageCAS masks are dilated ~1.5–1.9× in radius, the dominant correction is *shaving every vessel along its whole length* — and `_fillWithRemainder` hands the entire dilated seed out across the vessel classes while `maskToSeedCheck` blocks painting outside it by default. A UI affordance is not a mitigation. The mitigation is: **stop using the ImageCAS masks as seeds.**
4. **The proposed protocol and the implemented platform are two different systems**: 4 classes not 14, keyboard shortcuts capped at 9, flagging on a pooled `mean_dice`, no parent field, no fusion or arbitration concept at all, no annotation-protocol versioning, no per-voxel provenance — and the QA scorer "has never run on real label volumes".
5. **Resourcing: take §0 Option 1, reshaped.** ImageCAS-X cost 470 analyst-hours for 800 cases with a purpose-built tool; reproducing it (Option 2) costs this team ~1,500–2,200 hours. Option 3 costs the same as Option 1 and buys a structurally invalid verification. Option 1's headline deliverable must **not** be the 200 excluded scans — those were excluded as non-diagnostic (114 motion, 76 step artefact, 7 poor contrast) — but a blind, stratified 60-case independent re-annotation. ~250–330 annotator-hours, one term, with slack.

## 2. Errors found, ranked by what they change

### E1. Kappa sample sizes applied to a continuous per-class overlap measure — settled: this is a category error

**What the vault says.** `Proposed changes to the training plan.md:252-270`, marked **[verified]**: "Sim & Wright's Table 8 settles the sizing question, and the answer is uncomfortable" — 13 cases to show agreement beats chance, 126 for κ ≥ .80 rejecting below .60, 503 for κ ≥ .70 rejecting below .60, "roughly triple at 10 % prevalence" — concluding "**size the overlap set for the common branches**, and report the rare ones as wide-interval estimates rather than measured ceilings. No affordable duplicate rate will measure them."

**Part 1 — the numbers: unreached, but plausible; the [verified] marker is not earned.**
- The vault contradicts itself three-to-one on whether the paper was ever opened. `Sizing the overlap set and arbitrating disagreements.md:83-100`: "**I could not access it**… every secondary source confirms the paper *contains* the needed tables, none reproduces the numbers." `Class schema/Handover/Annotation protocol evidence.md:37-42`: "Not accessed… Retrieve before finalising the overlap-set size." `Verification log.md:210`: entries 21–22 are papers whose "quantitative values should not be quoted… Sim & Wright requires institutional access or author reprint." Only `Verification log.md:315-360` (entry 21) claims Table 8 was later "read in full through the UofT proxy."
- **I could not reach Table 8.** OUP (`academic.oup.com/ptj/article/85/3/257/2805022`) serves abstract only; Scribd and Academia.edu and ResearchGate all return metadata or HTTP 403. **Unreachable, not confirmed.**
- Two independent plausibility checks say the transcription is probably right. (a) *Internal arithmetic*: kappa power scales as 1/Δ², and the vault's own rows give 503/126 = 3.99 ≈ (0.20/0.10)² — exactly the expected ratio for halving the effect size. The "13 subjects" figure is the one universally attributed to this paper. (b) *An independent reproduction of the same family of tables*: Bujang & Baharum, *Guidelines of the minimum sample size requirements for Kappa agreement test* (Epidemiol Biostat Public Health, open access), computed from Flack et al. (1988) at ≥80% power, α<0.05, reports minimum sample sizes ranging **2 to 698** when marginal rating frequencies are equal, and instructs multiplying by two when they are not. The vault's 126–589 figures at p = .50 sit inside that range, and its 335–1,519 figures at p = .10/.90 are consistent with the unequal-marginal doubling. Nothing suggests the numbers are fabricated.
- **Ruling on provenance:** downgrade the §2.5 row from **[verified]** to **[reported]** until someone pastes the table into the log with the page number, or re-anchor the categorical calculation on Bujang & Baharum, which is open access and reproduces the arithmetic. Three vault documents saying "not accessed" against one saying "read via proxy" is not a verified citation.

**Part 2 — the application: a category error, and here is exactly where it bites.**

The overlap set's output is not a categorical rating. It is this, and only this:

- `server/girder_segqueue/scoring.py:110-130` `scoreDuplicate` → `src/segtrain/metrics.py:170-209` `agreement()` → a **continuous per-class Dice** (plus HD95 and per-class voxel counts) and a pooled `mean_dice`.
- `src/segqueue/policy.py:85-87` flags on `gold_dice_flag = 0.70` / `duplicate_dice_flag = 0.70`, compared against that mean.
- **There is no kappa computed anywhere in `src/`, `server/` or `slicer/`.** I grepped. The statistic Table 8 sizes does not exist in this system.

Three concrete consequences of the mismatch:

1. **The rating unit is never stated, and it changes the answer by six orders of magnitude.** Sim & Wright's N counts *subjects* — one categorical judgement per subject. If the unit is "is IM present in this scan", N = scans and 126 is affordable. If someone reads the table as applying to the per-voxel class assignment that the overlap set actually produces, N = voxels, 13 voxels would "suffice", and the number is nonsense. Worse, voxels are massively spatially autocorrelated, so a voxel-level kappa has no valid standard error at all. §2.5 must say "one judgement per scan per class" in the sentence that quotes the table.
2. **A per-class Dice study is sized by a variance calculation, not a power table** — `n ≈ (1.96·SD / half-width)²` for a CI of a given width on the mean per-class agreement. That calculation is available **right now** from ImageCAS-X's own published means and SDs (`How well two annotators agree on per-branch coronary labels.md:33-49`):

| Class | ImageCAS-X inter-observer DSC ± SD | Paired cases for ±2 pt 95% CI | for ±5 pt |
|---|---|---|---|
| RCA | 95.3 ± 5.0 | 24 | 4 |
| LAD | 92.3 ± 6.7 | 43 | 7 |
| LM | 91.9 ± 13.7 | 180 | 29 |
| R-PLA | 83.6 ± 18.6 | 332 | 53 |
| LCx | 84.8 ± 19.8 | 377 | 60 |
| D1 | 79.9 ± 28.7 | 791 | 127 |
| OM1 | 74.1 ± 32.3 | 1,002 | 160 |
| L-PLA | 70.9 ± 27.2 | 710 **positives** ⇒ ≈14,000 duplicated cases at 5% prevalence | 114 positives ⇒ ≈2,280 cases |

   This changes the decision in a way Table 8 cannot: **the vault draws the affordable/unaffordable line in the wrong place.** §2.5 sorts classes into "common branches — measurable" and "dominance-dependent — not". The variance table says LCx (377), D1 (791) and OM1 (1,002) are *also* unmeasurable to ±2 points at any plausible duplicate volume, and those are exactly the classes §2.5 counts as common. The honest tiering is three tiers, not two: **RCA/LAD measurable to ±2 pts in tens of cases; LM/LCx/R-PDA/R-PLA to ±5 pts in ~50–60; D1/D2/OM1/OM2/IM to ±5 pts in ~130–160; L-PDA/L-PLA at no achievable n.** That tiering is what the duplicate rate and the acceptance thresholds should be built on.
3. **Kappa is still right for one thing, and the source note knew it — the consolidation lost it.** `Sizing…md:88-96` correctly identifies presence/absence per class and dominance as the genuinely categorical, Dice-invisible error mode (ImageCAS-X presence agreement 87.5–100%, worst on the variably-present branches), and `Annotation and label efficiency/Proposed changes.md:43-55` proposes a **separate, deliberately-sized 30–50-case blind-paired mini-study** for it. §2.5 dropped that qualification and now reads as if Table 8 sizes the whole overlap set. The source note is right; the consolidation is wrong.

**Part 3 — a quantitative error inside the argument.** §2.5:266: "Your dominance-dependent branches sit near 1 %, past the end of the table." §3.2:308 repeats "under 1 % of cases". The vault's own numbers say ~5%: ImageCAS-X reports 41 left / 30 co-dominant of 800 (§2.2:220) = 5.1% / 3.75%; L-PDA nn = 8/160 and L-PLA nn = 9/160 in the test set = 5.0% / 5.6%; `Sizing…md:61` uses "~5%". The 1% figure is **wrong by 5×**, and it is deployed precisely to argue the requirement is off the end of the table — so the error flatters the pessimism and makes an already-correct conclusion look better-founded than it is.

**Fix.** Rewrite §2.5's sizing paragraph as two separate statements with two separate instruments:
- **(a) Continuous per-class agreement (what the platform measures).** Size from the variance table above; publish the three-tier structure; set the duplicate rate from tier 2's requirement; state that tier 3 classes are reported pooled across the programme's full runtime *with explicit confidence intervals*, and that tier 4 (L-PDA/L-PLA) are reported as point estimates with n, never as ceilings. Recompute the table from *our* SDs once the overlap set reaches 30 cases.
- **(b) Categorical presence/dominance (a separate, small study).** One 40–60-case blind-paired study, **stratified toward known or predicted left/co-dominant hearts**, sized on Bujang & Baharum or on Sim & Wright once someone actually opens it, with the rating unit written down as "one judgement per scan per class". Fold this into the same 60 cases as (a) by stratifying that set — it is the same annotations scored two ways, so it costs nothing extra. Nothing in the vault notices that these two studies can be one set of cases.

### E2. Intersection auto-accept systematically erodes vessels, and inverts its own precedent

**What the vault says.** `Proposed changes…md:283-284`; `Annotation and label efficiency/Proposed changes.md:57-67`; `Sizing…md:174-177`: "where two blind annotations already agree per class at or above the human ceiling, **accept the intersection and skip review**", modelled on FIVES.

**Why it is unsound.**
- **The arithmetic is disqualifying.** For two masks of equal volume V, Dice = 2|A∩B| / 2V = |A∩B| / V. **The intersection's volume, as a fraction of each annotator's, equals the Dice coefficient.** At ImageCAS-X's own measured ceilings the intersection therefore deletes, by construction, on every auto-accepted case: **7.2%** of the merged tree, 8.1% of LM, 15.2% of LCx, 16.4% of R-PLA, 20.1% of D1, **25.9% of OM1**, 24.9% of L-PDA, **29.1% of L-PLA**. And the deletion is not uniform noise — it is the outer voxel shell plus whichever distal extent each rater trimmed differently. On a 3–4-voxel-diameter tube, shaving the shell can **disconnect** the vessel, converting a benign boundary disagreement into a Betti-0 error and a dropped distal branch. That is precisely the failure the plan's Evaluation section says it exists to catch (`Training plan.md:178-183`: Dice "barely notices a missing distal branch"). It also biases every downstream lumen-diameter and stenosis measurement low, systematically, in the same direction, on the majority of cases — the single bias this project cannot afford.
- **FIVES does not support the proposal; it says the opposite.** Verified in the primary source (Jin K et al., *FIVES*, Sci Data 2022;9:475, read via PMC9352679): "The pixels annotated by the 2 annotators in common were included as the final ground truth" — **followed immediately by** "After being annotated by junior annotators, the images were further reviewed by senior annotators to correct errors", with senior discussion on genuine disagreement. FIVES used intersection as the *starting point for senior review of every single image*. The vault's own non-expert note records this correctly (`Can non-experts label vessels as well as experts.md:70-74`); the proposal inverts it into a mechanism for skipping review.
- **FIVES's regime is not ours, in three ways.** Its junior–junior inter-annotator Dice was **0.9241** (intra 0.9679, senior–junior 0.9608) on 2D fundus vessels — intersection there costs ~8%. Our side-branch ceilings are 0.71–0.85. FIVES annotated **2D** images where a one-pixel shell is a small fraction of a vessel cross-section; a 3-voxel coronary tube loses a third of its cross-section to the same erosion. And FIVES **explicitly refused model pre-annotation** because the best algorithm reached only ~0.82 Dice, judged too weak to seed ground truth (`Can non-experts…md:91-93`) — which is exactly the Dice the Asclepius binary model is calibrated to (`Training plan.md:185`).
- **A second independent group reaches the same conclusion about thin vessels.** The FOVEA dataset paper (Sci Data 2025, `s41597-025-04965-2`) reportedly states that intersection is viable ground truth for thicker vessels but degrades on the thinnest, where a two-pixel annotation offset can leave the binary masks not overlapping at all. I could **not** verify that quote — Nature redirected to an auth wall — so I do not rely on it; but it is 10 minutes of someone's time to retrieve, and it would convert E2 from an arithmetic argument into a cited one.
- **The vault already contains the correct answer, and §2.5 was written without reading it.** `Fusing multiple annotations and learning from noisy labels.md:189-199` concludes, on Karimi et al. 2020's head-to-head (majority vote 0.86/0.87 vs STAPLE 0.84/0.86 vs annotator-confusion estimation 0.92/0.93, catastrophic-error rate 0.01 vs 0.03): prefer **majority vote** over plain STAPLE for this project, and notes that STAPLE's per-rater sensitivity/specificity model is not validated on classes that are legitimately empty in most cases — a real objection for L-PDA/L-PLA. Nothing in that note endorses intersection. Two notes in the same folder give contradictory fusion advice and the consolidation picked the wrong one.
- **Interaction with E3 makes it worse, not better.** If the seed is systematically *dilated* (§E3), one might hope intersection erosion partially cancels it. It does not, reliably: both annotators inherit the *same* dilated seed, so their agreement is inflated by the shared error, the intersection is taken from an already-wrong starting boundary, and the net bias is uncontrolled and case-dependent. Two uncontrolled biases in opposite directions are not a correction; they are an unmeasurable label.

**Fix.** Delete intersection auto-accept. Replace with:
1. On a duplicate pair, run the flag set: per-class Dice against that class's tier threshold, **presence disagreement on any class**, dominance disagreement, and (once built) topology.
2. Where every class clears its threshold, take **one annotator's annotation whole** — randomised which, and recorded. No erosion, the same reviewer saving, and the training label stays an actual human product rather than a synthetic shrunken one.
3. Where any class fails, route to the adjudicator, and route them to the **symmetric difference** rather than the whole case, so their time goes where agreement actually failed.
4. Note that **at n = 2 raters, majority vote degenerates to intersection**, so the fusion note's recommendation is only available with a third rater. If a fused label is genuinely wanted, budget the third rater; otherwise union-with-arbitration on the difference region is the only n = 2 fusion that is not systematically biased in one direction.

### E3. Seeding bias: the mitigation named in §1.5 is aimed at the wrong error, and the shipped tool enforces the bias

**What the vault says.** `Proposed changes…md:143-153`: ImageCAS's masks disagree with ImageCAS-X's re-annotation at **41.8% Dice** and include plaque, pulmonary vessels and coronary veins; "those same masks seed the presegmentations annotators are handed, so the bias propagates into whatever they produce. **The annotation UI must make deleting a seed voxel as cheap as drawing one.**"

**What is actually true, in the code.**
- The seed shipped to annotators today **is** the original source-dataset mask: `src/segqueue/protocol.py:98-99` — `ASSET_SEED = "seed"`, "A pre-existing binary coronary lumen mask **from the source dataset**"; uploaded per case at `server/girder_segqueue/ingest.py:262`. `Training plan.md:129` *intends* the binary model's predictions to be the seed, but that is not what is implemented, and a model trained on those masks inherits the same bias anyway.
- `slicer/SegQueue/SegQueue.py:1684-1689` — `maskToSeedCheck = QCheckBox("Only let me paint inside the mask")`, `setChecked(True)`; at :2356-2364 this sets `EditAllowedInsideSingleSegment` on the seed segment. **By default the annotator cannot paint a voxel the seed does not already contain.** Deleting is already free — scissors explicitly bypass both masks ("Trimming ignores it", and the `onMaskingChanged` docstring at :2340-2350 explains why). So §1.5's requested affordance is *already satisfied* in the direction it names, and *absent* in the direction it does not.
- `_fillWithRemainder` (:2402-2450) is a **partition of the seed**: the first vessel opened receives the entire tree, the annotator trims, and the trimmings go to the next vessel — "Nothing they remove is ever lost -- it is handed to the vessel after." **Every seed voxel therefore ends in some coronary class unless explicitly deleted from the last one.** A seed containing a coronary vein or a pulmonary vessel produces a coronary label for it *by construction*.

**What the dilation figure does to this picture — and it is the part §1.5 has not realised.** If the original masks are dilated roughly **1.5–1.9× in radius** relative to careful re-annotation, then the dominant correction is not deleting stray objects. It is **shaving the outer 30–50% of the radius off every vessel, along its entire length, on every case.** Three consequences the plan has not costed:

1. **The work is per-slice boundary erosion, and the tool is not built for it.** SegQueue's trimming gesture is 3D Scissors — a cut *across* a tree to separate branches, which is what "splitting a tree is minutes" refers to. Shaving a radius *along* a vessel is a different operation entirely: it is the 20 min/case "lumen correction" phase of CoronaryExplorer, not the 15 min/case centerline phase. So the seeded-annotation time estimate is not "minutes" — it is dominated by exactly the phase a dilated seed makes longer, and `What tools exist for semi-automatic branch splitting of vessel segmentations.md` already flags this ("CoronaryExplorer's time budget assumes the input binary lumen mask is already correct… If the mask is a model prediction with systematic errors the 'lumen correction' phase will be longer").
2. **Mask-confinement is worse than neutral here, and the intuition that it is harmless is wrong.** "Only paint inside the mask" is harmless if the seed is a *superset* of truth, because then you only ever need to subtract. A dilated seed *is* a superset, so the confinement never blocks the needed edit — which sounds fine, and is exactly why it is dangerous: it makes the workflow feel correct while removing the annotator's only means of noticing the places where the seed is *not* a superset (a missed distal branch, an occluded segment the mask stops short of). The default guarantees that one whole class of seed error — omission — can never be corrected, and omission of small distal branches is the error mode Karimi's mechanism predicts (`Detecting a drifting or bad annotator.md:36-70`: 18% of lesions missed on a first pass, systematically the smaller and fainter ones).
3. **A uniform radial shave is the single most learnable, most confirmable bias there is.** An annotator who under-shaves by a consistent amount produces labels that are wrong in one direction on every vessel of every case, and Shwartzman et al. (arXiv:1906.11872, as reported at `Fusing…md:151-180`) measured exactly this: a consistent one-directional rater bias is not averaged away by training — it is **amplified** in the network's predictions relative to the raw label difference. Inter-rater agreement cannot see it, because both annotators inherit the same seed and will agree with each other while both being wrong. The vault states this correctly at `Seeding annotation…md:78-82`; §1.5's one-sentence UI mitigation does not act on it.

**What the literature actually supports as a mitigation.** The vault's two cited studies are the right two and its synthesis is honest (`Seeding annotation…md:22-93`): Mikulová et al. (arXiv:2306.09307, dependency parsing) found pre-annotation cost no accuracy (LAS 95.0 pre-parsed vs 94.5 from scratch), saved 1.7× time, and *raised* inter-annotator agreement; Shwartzman et al. found systematic seed bias propagates and amplifies. **No measured magnitude of anchoring bias in seeded medical-image annotation exists** — I searched for one and did not find it, and the vault does not claim one. That absence *is* the finding: the mitigation is being chosen without an effect size, so the protocol must measure its own rather than assume a checkbox handles it.

**Fix — state it plainly, in priority order.**
1. **Stop seeding from the ImageCAS masks.** This is the whole answer to §1.5 and the plan should say so instead of prescribing a UI behaviour. A seed at 41.8% Dice against careful re-annotation, systematically dilated, containing plaque, coronary veins and pulmonary vessels, is the worst available seed — and ImageCAS-X's centerline-derived lumen for 800 cases under CC BY 4.0 is sitting right there. Seed from ImageCAS-X, or from a binary model retrained on ImageCAS-X's lumen, and never from the original masks.
2. **Per-class seed-adherence telemetry at submit time** — cheapest high-value change in the whole slice. The extension holds both the seed segment and the final segments, and `SubmissionMeta.voxel_counts` (`protocol.py:424`) already ships per-class counts computed client-side. Add three integers per class: `|final ∩ seed|`, `|final \ seed|` (added beyond the seed), `|seed \ ∪final|` (deleted). That yields, free, on every case, with no duplicate and no model inference: a per-annotator per-class **signed** seed-adherence time series. An annotator whose `final \ seed` is ~0 across 40 cases is colouring in, not annotating; an annotator whose `seed \ ∪final` fraction is well below the cohort median is under-shaving a dilated seed. This is simultaneously §2.5's requested "missed-structure check against the model's own presegmentation" and its requested "signed rather than absolute per-class bias".
3. **A randomised seedless subset** — the only design that *detects* anchoring rather than assuming it away. `ingest.py:189, 262` already supports `--no-seed`, but only as a global flag for an entire ingest run; there is no per-case randomisation. Make it per case at ~5%, record the assignment, and compare per-class Dice, vessel radius and distal extent on seeded vs seedless cases **by the same annotator**. `AssignmentInfo.has_seed` (`protocol.py:294-299`) is visible to the annotator — unavoidable, and harmless for this design, since the absence of a seed cannot be hidden. What must be blind is nothing; what must be random is which cases.
4. **A radius/volume check against the seed, per class.** Given a known dilation direction, the cheapest single QC rule in the programme is: flag any submission whose per-class volume is above some fraction of the seed's volume for that territory. It needs no duplicate, no gold, and no topology. It is not in the vault.
5. **Randomised seed quality is not worth building** (one seed per case from the source dataset; no mechanism exists). The seedless arm answers the same question.

### E4. The topological validator cannot be specified as written, and its false-positive claim is wrong

**What the vault says.** `Proposed changes…md:279-282`: "**Topological validation needs no duplicate at all** — a valid tree, one parent per segment, no cycles, plausible parent-child relations. Under 5 seconds a case. Given that duplicates cannot cover the rare classes, this is the cheaper protection and should be built first." Source note `Quality control rules that do not need duplicate assignment.md:14-36`: "Every labeled segment has exactly one parent, except the root (LM)"; "Every segment belongs to the correct domain (left or right subtree from LM)"; "**False-positive risk: Very low if the transition matrix is correct. A topological error is genuinely an error.**"

**What is wrong.**
- **"Left or right subtree from LM" is anatomically false.** The RCA is not a descendant of the LM. The coronary tree is two (sometimes three) independent trees from two ostia — the plan itself says so (`Training plan.md:145-147`: "The coronary tree is two or three disconnected trees"). So "one parent except LM" is wrong twice: RCA also has no parent, and a single-root assumption flags every correct annotation.
- **The validator misses the dominant error mode.** D1 vs D2 and OM1 vs OM2 are distinguished by *ordinal position along the parent*, not by topology; a swap is topologically legal. Those are exactly where ImageCAS-X's analysts disagree most (D1 79.9 ± 28.7, OM1 74.1 ± 32.3). LAD↔LCx confusion in a case with an unusual take-off is also topologically legal — both descend from LM. The validator catches the errors nobody makes and misses the ones everybody makes.
- **"Other" has no defined parent**, so any voxel an annotator cannot place can be dumped there and pass. One of 14 classes is a validator bypass.
- **False-positive risk is not low.** A real coronary tree in a real CCTA is routinely disconnected *in the voxel mask*: a total occlusion, a motion-artefact gap in the mid RCA, a calcified segment excluded from the lumen. ImageCAS-X's measured **inter-observer Betti error is 0.2–0.4** (`Proposed changes…md:244`) — two careful humans differ topologically on 20–40% of cases. A hard tree check would flag a large fraction of correct work. The source note sets the right register ("flag, do not reject", `Quality control rules…md:116`); §2.5 loses it when it promotes topology to "the cheaper protection… build it first".
- **The tree does not exist in the data model.** `src/segqueue/protocol.py:164-205` — `SegmentSpec` carries `name`, `label`, `color`, `required`, `hint`. **No parent field. No transition table.** `ProjectConfig` (:208-256) has no hierarchy either. A validator would have to *infer* parentage from voxel adjacency in a 512×512×~250 multiclass label map where 1–2-voxel-wide vessels run parallel and touch (LAD against the great cardiac vein; LCx against the coronary sinus), so spurious adjacencies are the norm, not the exception. **ImageCAS-X avoids this entirely by naming a centerline and deriving voxels by nearest-centerline assignment** (`Proposed changes…md:195-197`), where the tree is explicit and validation is trivial. So: **topological validation is cheap if and only if centerline-first labelling is adopted first.** On painted voxels it is neither cheap nor unambiguous, and "under 5 seconds a case" is asserted with no measurement.
- **The recommended file does not exist.** `Quality control rules…md:114` says "Add it to `src/segqueue/scoring.py`." There is no such file; scoring lives in `server/girder_segqueue/scoring.py`. And `src/segqueue/segcheck.py:89-150` — the module that *does* run per-submission checks on both client and server — deliberately takes only `{segment name: voxel count}` plus a geometry record, "so the rules over those summaries mean one implementation, tested once, **with no numpy in sight**" (:14-15). A topological validator cannot live there without breaking the one design property that makes the client and server checks provably identical.

**Answer to "what IS a plausible parent-child relation across dominance variants?"** It is specifiable, and the ImageCAS-X schema makes it *easier* than the vault realises, which is worth saying explicitly as a point in that schema's favour: **dominance is encoded in the class name** (R-PDA vs L-PDA, R-PLA vs L-PLA), so once the class is chosen the parent is determined. The full table:

`roots = {LM, RCA}` · `LM → {LAD, LCx, IM}` · `LAD → {D1, D2}` · `LCx → {OM1, OM2, L-PDA, L-PLA}` · `RCA → {R-PDA, R-PLA}` · `Other`: exempt.

with these stated exceptions: IM is legal only where LM trifurcates (and an IM parented on proximal LAD is a *naming* judgement, not a topology violation — do not flag it); a class may be disconnected from its parent in the voxel mask for genuine reasons (occlusion, artefact gap), so disconnection is a warning not an error; and co-dominance legitimately produces both R- and L- variants of PDA/PLA in one case.

**Fix.** (a) Reorder — adopt centerline-first labelling before promising topological validation, and specify the validator over the centerline graph. (b) Ship the parent-child table above as data in `SegmentSpec`/`ProjectConfig`, not as prose. (c) State in §2.5 that the validator catches structural violations and **not** ordinal naming errors, which remain duplicate-only. (d) Flag, never reject; calibrate the flag rate on 100 ImageCAS-X cases before live use, as the source note already says. (e) Restore the source note's closing sentence, which §2.5 dropped: "**None of this replaces duplicates** for detecting subtle boundary disagreements."

### E5. Implemented protocol ≠ proposed protocol, in five specific ways

None are literature errors; all change what has to be built.

| | Proposal | What the code actually does |
|---|---|---|

### E6. Two citation errors and one overstated claim in the label-efficiency section

- **Active learning — author misattributed.** `Active learning strategies for 3D medical segmentation do not consistently beat random sampling.md:138` and `Annotation and label efficiency/Proposed changes.md:125` attribute arXiv:2207.00845 to "**Schlemper J, et al.**". It is **Burmeister J-M, Fernandez Rosas M, Hagemann J, Kordt J, Blum J, Shabo S, Bergner B, Lippert C**, *Less Is More: A Comparison of Active Learning Strategies for 3D Medical Image Segmentation* (verified on the arXiv abstract page). **The conclusion holds** — the paper's own finding is that "both random and strided sampling act as strong baselines" with no specialised strategy clearly superior, across three Medical Segmentation Decathlon datasets. Two secondary problems: the note's six-strategy list (LCUS/EntrUS/StrRS/ClustRS/DistRS/RandS) and its per-dataset breakdown ("Heart… Hippocampus… Prostate") do not match the abstract, which names **strided sampling** as the paper's novel contribution and the note omits it entirely; and the sentence the note presents as "verbatim from the paper" ("most query strategies performed similarly on the three datasets studied, and no strategy outperformed random sampling (RandS) by a large margin") does not appear in the text I could reach. **Treat the strategy list and per-dataset breakdown as unverified; the headline conclusion as verified.** The recommendation — do not build active learning; random or dominance-stratified selection is defensible, and any attempt must be measured against a random baseline — is correct, and **it does save the project a whole workstream.** Cheapest win in this slice.
- **Partial-annotation self-training is overstated in the consolidation.** `Proposed changes…md:351` (experiment 5): "24.29 % of branches labelled reached parity with full annotation via self-training." I reached the arXiv abstract for Zhang Z, Zhang X, Qi Y, Yang G, *Partial Vessels Annotation-based Coronary Artery Segmentation with Self-training and Prototype Learning*, MICCAI 2023 (arXiv:2307.04472). The actual claim is narrower: the method "outperforms the competing methods under PVA (24.29% vessels), and achieves **comparable performance in trunk continuity** with the baseline model using full annotation (100% vessels)." **Parity is claimed for trunk continuity only — not overall segmentation, and not per branch.** The folder note is honest about the limitation (`Seeding annotation…md:100-125`: abstract only, "retrieve the full text before relying on the number"); the consolidation dropped the qualifier. Dataset, case count and institution are still unstated. It stays **[unverified]** and is worth one scheduled experiment, but "the single largest lever available on annotator hours" is not supported by a trunk-continuity result — trunks are the part this project already gets right (RCA 95.3, LAD 92.3).
- **"20–30 cases to proficiency" rests on a study whose annotators never agreed.** `How much training and how fast does annotator agreement improve.md:11-21` gives **no author, DOI or arXiv ID** for either of its two studies — they are not merely [unverified], they are **uncitable as written**. I located the one it describes: *Endoscopic sleeve gastroplasty video annotation: analysis of the learning curve of three annotators to achieve procedure proficiency* (PMC13336686). The 28.40 figure is real, as is n = 36 for the "other sutures" phase (first suture 21, end-phase features 14–31). Two facts the vault omits change its value: (i) the three annotators were an expert bariatric surgeon, a surgical resident, and **an engineering student with minimal expertise** — actually a well-matched population for an undergraduate team; and (ii) **pairwise Cohen's kappa was 0.440 / 0.469 / 0.585.** "Proficiency" is defined there as the annotators' own curves stabilising — verbatim: "the agreement on features among all annotators, with the agreement defined as achieved when the answers of the annotators, drawn into curves, were matching for multiple iterations" — i.e. stabilisation at a level of absolute agreement (κ ≈ 0.5, "moderate") that would be unacceptable here. Extrapolating "20–30 cases to proficiency, weight the first 15 lower in arbitration" from a study whose *proficient* annotators sat at κ ≈ 0.5 is a weak foundation. **No imaging-annotation-specific "cases to proficiency" measurement exists** — I searched and found none. The nearest real evidence points elsewhere: Skandarani 2021 found that **training depth** (months plus fine delineation guidelines) rather than case count closed the gap on hard structures (`Can non-experts…md:100-128`). Keep the 5-case gate (`policy.py:52`) and the "weight early cases lower" instinct, but derive the number from **our own** per-annotator learning curve against gold, which the platform can measure from case 1 — as `How much training…md`'s own closing section proposes.

### E7. Smaller factual and consistency errors

- **RCA is marked optional, for a wrong reason.** `server/girder_segqueue/settings.py:35-36`: `right_coronary_artery`, `required: False`, hint "May be small or absent in a left-dominant system." A left-dominant system still has an RCA; what flips is the origin of the PDA/PLA. The vault's own table has RCA present in **160/160** scans at 100.0% presence agreement — the most reliable structure in the dataset. `required: False` makes `segcheck.check_submission` (`segcheck.py:109-120`) downgrade a missing RCA from a blocking error to a warning, removing the check where it would almost always be correct.
- **A taper rule already ships, uncalibrated and unversioned.** `Proposed changes…md:198-200`: "A taper rule is still needed. Pick one… and calibrate on 5–10 cases before live annotation." `settings.py:39-44` already ships one, of the visibility kind: "Stop each branch where the lumen is no longer confidently distinguishable from surrounding tissue, and say so in the note rather than guessing." It is live. Either calibrate it or replace it, but the plan should not describe it as absent.
- **Overlap rate 20% vs 5%, contradictory across two vault documents.** `Handover:117-124` recommends "20 % of whatever we annotate, blind, randomly paired, not reviewed before scoring" (the ImageCAS-X design). §2.5:273-274 endorses SegQueue's 5% "as the operating design". `Sizing…md:44-54` contains a good reconciliation — a one-off study fraction versus a sustained continuous QA rate are not the same number — which §2.5 does not carry, so the two documents read as simply contradicting each other. State both rates with their distinct purposes.
- **`training_gate_size` does not exist.** `Sizing…md:22-24` cites it; the field is `training_gate_cases` (`policy.py:52`).
- **Two real platform bugs to pass on.** `policy.py:194-198`: when `gold_available` is False but `duplicate_available` is True, a roll below `gold_rate` falls through to `roll < gold_rate + duplicate_rate` and returns `DUPLICATE` — so with no gold cases loaded the **duplicate rate silently doubles to 10%**. And `scoring.py:133-139` `_partnerSubmission` returns the *first* other annotator's submission, so with `replicas_wanted > 2` the pairing is arbitrary and unrecorded.
- **A circularity in annotator certification that nobody has flagged.** `Handover:102-110` proposes certifying annotators against ImageCAS-X training-split labels. If ImageCAS-X becomes the training data (§0 Option 1) *and* the gold reference *and* the certification standard, a systematic ImageCAS-X convention error is invisible at every stage: annotators are trained to reproduce it, the gold check rewards reproducing it, and the model learns it. The note correctly says "ImageCAS-X labels are analyst labels, a reference and not ground truth" but does not draw the consequence. **Hold back a small clinician-adjudicated gold set that is not ImageCAS-X-derived.**

## 3. Weak reasoning / unsupported leaps

1. **"Splitting an existing tree is minutes; drawing one is an hour" (`Training plan.md:129`) is asserted, and the vault's own evidence contradicts the ratio.** The only measured coronary figure is CoronaryExplorer's **470 analyst-hours / 800 cases = 35.3 min/case** (200 h centerline + 270 h lumen), achieved by **four trained analysts using a purpose-built centerline-first Slicer extension with an automatic centerline tracer, on a pre-provided binary lumen mask**, with a lead analyst reviewing every case on top. So "minutes" is 35 minutes; and **no from-scratch coronary figure exists at all** — the "an hour" has no source. The vault's two real anchors for the *ratio* are Mikulová's 1.7× on text annotation and FIVES's 3–5 h/image on 2D fundus without seeding. The honest statement: seeded ≈ 35 min/case measured on this cohort with a purpose-built tool; unseeded unmeasured; ratio somewhere between 1.7× and much larger. SegQueue's tool is **not** centerline-first — it is a voxel partition of the seed — so 35.3 min/case is not even the right benchmark for it. **Measure it on 10 cases before any §0 option is costed.** The plan's most-quoted efficiency claim is currently its least-evidenced.
2. **The human ceiling is treated as a fixed constant when the vault's own note says it is an optimistic, seed-shared bound.** `How well two annotators agree…md:74-91` reasons carefully that both ImageCAS-X analysts started from the same machine pre-labels, so their measured agreement is an *upper* bound on independent annotation — and honestly marks that as inference, not stated fact. It also flags (:95-99) that the comparison may be lead-reviewed first set versus unreviewed second set, marked **Unverified**, which would make part of the "inter-observer" gap a review effect rather than human disagreement. Every threshold in §2.4, the whole intersection proposal, and my own variance table in E1 are pegged to this curve. It is the right curve; the caveats need to travel with the number into the plan, and currently they do not.
3. **Acceptance thresholds are pegged to a ceiling whose SDs exceed its gaps.** OM1 is 74.1 ± 32.3. `How well two annotators agree…md:59-64` reads this correctly as bimodal — mostly near-perfect, with a tail of vessel-identity swaps collapsing toward 0 — and recommends reporting "the fraction of cases below X" alongside the mean. §2.4 does not carry that recommendation. A per-class *mean* threshold on a bimodal distribution is close to meaningless, and it is exactly what the QA flag will compare against.
4. **"Under 5 seconds a case" and "very low false-positive risk" are both asserted without measurement** (E4).
5. **The rare-class conclusion is reached twice by two arguments, and neither is the load-bearing one.** Both the prevalence arithmetic (`Sizing…md:56-81`) and Table 8 conclude "no affordable duplicate rate stabilises L-PDA/L-PLA". The variance table in E1 shows *why*, in the statistic actually computed, and shows the problem reaching LCx, D1 and OM1 — which §2.5 places in the "measurable common branches" bucket. The right conclusion, drawn in the wrong place, by the wrong instrument.
6. **§2.5 endorses "SegQueue's 5 % gold" without noticing what gold now means.** A gold case requires `case['goldFileId']` (`scoring.py:86-95`). With ImageCAS-X imported, gold references exist for 800 cases, so 5% gold becomes nearly free — a genuine improvement nobody has stated. But it is also the circularity in E7's last bullet. Both facts belong in the proposal.

## 4. What's missing — extensions that would change a decision

### X1. The §0 resourcing decision, costed in annotator-hours

**Measured anchor.** ImageCAS-X: **470 analyst-hours / 800 cases = 35.3 min/case** (200 h centerline correction + 270 h lumen correction), four trained analysts, purpose-built centerline-first Slicer extension with an automatic centerline tracer, plus a lead analyst reviewing every case (not included in the 470).

**Two multipliers this project must apply to that number, and both are up.** (i) *Workforce*: undergraduates, not trained analysts; Skandarani's evidence is that brief training costs real accuracy on hard structures. (ii) *Tooling*: SegQueue is a voxel partition of a seed, not centerline-first, and has no automatic tracer; it is built and captioned for 4 classes with keyboard shortcuts capped at 9. **Assume 2–3× the analyst rate: 70–105 min/case for 14 classes.** This is an assumption, flagged as such, and it is the single number most worth measuring before committing (see §3 item 1). Capacity: 10 students × 4 h/week × 12 weeks ≈ **480 annotator-hours per term.**

**The 200 excluded scans are not a deliverable, and this changes Option 1's shape.** They were excluded as **non-diagnostic**: 114 motion, 76 step artefact, 7 poor contrast. §0 Option 1 as written ("redirect annotators to the 200 cases ImageCAS-X excluded") sends the least-experienced annotators at the worst scans in the cohort — where inter-rater agreement will be at its floor, where the seed will be at its worst, where the taper rule ("stop where the lumen is no longer confidently distinguishable") will fire on most of the tree, and where "annotator cannot see the vessel" is indistinguishable from "annotator missed the vessel" in every QA statistic the platform has. Budget a **10-case feasibility probe (~18 h)** and expect to abandon. If a subset *is* labellable, it is worth having as a deliberately hard held-out test set (as `Handover:139-141` suggests) — but it cannot be the programme's main output, and the plan should stop describing it as the obvious redirect.

**Recommendation: Option 1, reshaped.** Options 1 and 3 cost roughly the same; the difference is entirely what you get, and Option 2 costs 5–7× either. So the decision is Option 1 versus Option 3, and Option 3 loses on grounds of validity, not cost: annotators *correcting* ImageCAS-X labels, under the anchoring mechanism documented in E3 — with mask-confinement on by default and the whole seed partitioned across classes — will overwhelmingly confirm them. You would spend ~200 hours to learn nothing, and you would destroy ImageCAS-X as a held-out comparator in the process. **Explicitly reject Option 3.**

The annotation team's deliverable under Option 1 is four things, none of which is "1000 labels":
- **(a)** A **blind, stratified, independent 60-case per-branch re-annotation** of ImageCAS-X *training-split* cases, two raters each, scored three ways: rater-vs-rater (our team's own ceiling), rater-vs-ImageCAS-X (an *external* audit of ImageCAS-X's labels — which nobody has done, since ImageCAS-X's own inter-observer study shares its seed, its tool and its protocol), and per-class presence/dominance kappa.
- **(b)** The categorical study folded into (a) by stratifying the 60 toward left/co-dominant hearts — same annotations, scored twice, zero extra hours.
- **(c)** Targeted review of the cases where the trained multiclass model most disagrees with ImageCAS-X — the highest-yield audit per hour available, and the only one that finds label errors the import check would miss.
- **(d)** The 10-case probe on the excluded 200.

**And restate the contribution.** `Training plan.md:140` — "The contribution is the per-branch labelling" — is no longer true, and every sequencing decision in the plan inherits from it. The contribution that Option 1 actually produces is *the independent verification and inter-rater characterisation of per-branch coronary labelling by a non-expert workforce*, plus the model. That is publishable, it is not a duplicate of anything, and it costs one term rather than four.

### X2. Per-class Dice sizing as a standing calculation
The variance table in E1 belongs in the vault as the sizing instrument, recomputed from *our* measured SDs once the overlap set reaches 30 cases. It replaces Table 8 for the question the platform actually answers, and it is what decides whether the duplicate rate is 5% or 20%.

### X3. Intra-rater agreement — free, and entirely absent
`How well two annotators agree…md:139-147` identifies this correctly as a real gap: without it you cannot separate "this annotator is inconsistent" from "these two annotators disagree" — the difference between retraining a person and rewriting the guideline. FIVES measured it and the ordering is the diagnostic (intra 0.9679 > senior–junior 0.9608 > junior–junior 0.9241). SegQueue can get it almost free: re-serve a case the same annotator did 6+ weeks earlier, blind. The state machine already supports it — state lives on the *assignment*, not the case, precisely so one case can be out with N annotators (`states.py:1-11`). Nothing in §2.5 asks for it. **Cheapest missing measurement in the programme.**

### X4. Provenance per voxel — and the cheap version worth building now
Full per-voxel provenance (which annotator, which pass, seed-derived or hand-drawn, protocol version) is the right long-term answer and is recorded nowhere. The 95% version is E3's fix 2: three integers per class in `SubmissionMeta.voxel_counts`, computed client-side where the arrays already are. Build that; defer full provenance.

### X5. Annotation-protocol versioning — the gap that will silently corrupt the dataset
`ProjectConfig.protocol_version` (`protocol.py:230`) is the **wire** protocol version (`PROTOCOL_VERSION`), not the labelling-protocol version, and `instructions` + `segments` are a single **mutable** Girder setting (`settings.py:52-58`). So the day someone changes the taper rule, or adds a class, every past submission becomes unattributable to a rule, with no record that the rule changed and no way to select cases annotated under version N. §2.1 is *actively proposing* a mid-course schema change (4 → 14 classes). Over a 1000-case programme this is a dataset-integrity failure, not a nicety. **Fix:** a monotonic `annotation_protocol_version` in `ProjectConfig`, bumped on any change to `segments` or `instructions`, stamped onto every submission, with the setting change written to an append-only audit collection. Ten lines. Then decide the re-annotation policy *before* the change: which cases are redone, which get a mechanical remap (14 → 4 merges cleanly; 4 → 14 does not split, per `Proposed changes…md:190-191` — so the 4-class work already done can never be upgraded), and which are marked version N and excluded from version N+1 training. **This constraint alone is an argument for settling the schema before another case is annotated.**

### X6. Adjudication authority — an open question with no owner
`Handover:146-147` flags it honestly: "Whether a clinician is available as senior reviewer; the protocol degrades to 'lead annotator' otherwise." It is upstream of the whole arbitration design and `states.py` has no reviewer-authority tier at all — only `APPROVE`/`REJECT`. Three things need writing down: who the single adjudicator is and what happens in their absence; that an adjudicated label carries a *distinct provenance class* (not "annotator 3"); and a rule for what to do when the adjudicator's own overturn rate is high — which is evidence about the guideline, not about the annotators.

### X7. Recruitment, expertise and instructions — where the evidence is unambiguous and the plan does not act
Rädsch et al. 2023 (Nat Mach Intell 5:273–283): **exemplary pictures** cut severe errors by a median 33.9%, odds of a severe error **0.37× (CI 0.28–0.50)** versus minimal text, while extended text alone did nothing ("no statistically significant difference"); professional annotators' odds of a severe error were 0.09× (CI 0.06–0.12) those of crowdworkers; and a severe error is defined as "an annotation with a metric score equal to zero" — once the object was correctly identified there was no significant DSC difference. Skandarani 2021: 30 minutes of training costs ~6 Dice points on hard structures; months plus fine delineation guidelines costs nothing. FIVES: **pass/fail onboarding on 5 test images, and annotators who failed did not proceed** — whereas SegQueue's 5-case gate merely forces *review* (`policy.py:144-145` returns rate 1.0) and excludes nobody. Three additions: (a) budget a **picture-first guideline** — exemplars per class including rare and ambiguous cases — as a named deliverable, because it is the one intervention with a measured effect size and prose alone is measurably worthless; (b) make the 5-case gate **pass/fail** against ImageCAS-X reference cases with a stated per-class margin; (c) recruit for **duration of commitment** over credential, since training depth is what buys accuracy and undergraduate attrition is the platform's own stated top risk (`policy.py:15-17`).

### X8. Prove the scoring path before redesigning it
`docs/SERVER-HANDOFF.md:21, 162-164`. Run `segqueue-score` on ~20 ImageCAS-X cases with ImageCAS-X as gold; read the `per_structure` output; then decide the flag design. One day, and it precedes all four §2.5 QA changes.
